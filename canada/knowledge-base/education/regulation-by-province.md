# Private and alternative schooling: the rules in each province and territory

Agent 03 · researched 2026-10-30 · Phase 1. Records cited in backticks are in
`regulation/` (rules) and `schools/` (institutions). Sources and access dates
are in each record. This is research, not legal advice.

## How to read this

Canada has no national school law. Each province and territory decides what a
non-public school is, whether it gets public money, who inspects it and whether
it can award the provincial diploma. For a family choosing an alternative
school, four distinctions decide most practical questions:

| Term used here | Meaning | Example records |
|---|---|---|
| **Independent / private school** | A school run by a non-government body that the province recognises (registered, permitted, or classified) and that children attend in place of public school | `school-bc-north-vancouver-vancouver-waldorf-school`, `school-on-vaughan-toronto-waldorf-school` |
| **Registered private school (no oversight)** | Recognised only by filing a notice; no inspection unless it grants diploma credits (Ontario) | `school-on-toronto-waldorf-academy` |
| **Homeschooling provider** | A school or authority that registers or supervises home-educated children, sometimes with funding (B.C. registration, Alberta supervision) | `school-ab-calgary-banbury-crossroads-school`, `school-ab-calgary-phoenix-education-foundation` |
| **Online / distributed-learning school** | A school that enrols the child and supervises learning at a distance through certified teachers; can award the diploma | `school-bc-vancouver-selfdesign-learning-community`, `school-on-bayfield-virtual-high-school` |
| **Educational support organisation** | Classes, co-ops, networks that support home learners but do not enrol them | none recorded in Phase 1 |

## British Columbia

Record: `nat-bc-education-independent-schools-and-home-learning`.

- **Legal term.** Independent school under the *Independent School Act*. The
  ministry classifies each school by grade band into **Group 1–4**, published
  in the school-contact register (`allindependentschools.csv`, field "Group
  Classification").
- **Groups.** Group 1: non-profit authority, B.C.-certified teachers and
  principal, ministry instructional-time and program requirements, Grades 10–12
  meet graduation requirements, provincial assessments, operating cost not above
  the local district grant; receives **50 %** of the local per-student grant.
  Group 2: same, but costs more per student; receives **35 %**. Group 3: at
  least half the students eligible residents, evaluated every two years, no
  grant. Group 4: mostly international students, bond, no grant; certified
  teachers needed to offer the Dogwood.
- **Inspection.** Groups 1 and 2: inspected at least every two years and a full
  external evaluation at least every six.
- **Diploma.** The B.C. Certificate of Graduation (Dogwood) is available through
  Group 1, 2 and 4 schools and through online learning schools. Homeschoolers
  cannot receive it.
- **Online learning.** "Distributed learning" was renamed **online learning**
  (Bill 8, 2020; in force 2023). The ministry lists 15 *independent* online
  learning schools (Group 1, under agreement with the minister), including
  `school-bc-vancouver-selfdesign-learning-community` and the online arm of
  `school-bc-victoria-oak-and-orca-bioregional-school`. Learning is supervised
  by a B.C. teacher; the student can graduate with a Dogwood.
- **Homeschooling.** Register each child with a public school or a willing
  independent school by **30 September**. Parents direct everything; the school
  cannot supervise or approve the programme; no inspection; no Dogwood. The
  registering school (not the family) receives $250 (public) or $175 (Group 1/2).
  Grades 10–12 can mix homeschool registration with online courses.
- **What this means for an alternative-minded family.** B.C. is the most
  favourable province in the study: public money lowers fees at Group 1 Waldorf
  and Montessori schools (Nelson Waldorf $6,240 for 2026-27,
  `school-bc-nelson-nelson-waldorf-school`), and funded online schools offer a
  learner-directed path to a recognised diploma. Eligibility for the funded
  programmes depends on being a B.C.-resident "eligible" student.

## Alberta

Record: `nat-ab-education-independent-schools-and-home-education`.

- **Legal term.** Alberta now calls private schools **independent schools**
  (*Education Act* s.29). Three tiers: **registered** (no funding; certificated
  teachers and the Alberta programs of study not required), **accredited
  non-funded** (certificated teachers), **accredited funded** (programs of
  study, leadership-certified principal, grant after normally one unfunded
  year). All submit an operating plan by 15 May; funded schools are monitored
  yearly and visited.
- **Funding.** About 70 % of public base-instruction rates is widely reported
  (Cardus, Fraser Institute) but was not found on alberta.ca; transportation
  funding is 70 % of the public rate (Grades 1–12).
- **Home education.** Either **supervised** by a willing public, separate,
  francophone board or accredited funded independent school (accepted before
  30 September; parents can claim their **50 %** share of the home-education
  grant; at least two evaluations per year by certificated teachers), or
  **notification-only** to the Minister (no funding, no plan submitted).
  Alberta Education's directory flags which authorities supervise home
  education, offer shared responsibility (blended) and online learning.
- **Diploma and online.** Not confirmed on the pages read; to research in
  Phase 2.
- **Implication.** Alberta combines private alternatives
  (`school-ab-calgary-calgary-waldorf-school`,
  `school-ab-calgary-banbury-crossroads-school`) with a supervised,
  partly-funded home-education model that private authorities can run.

## Ontario

Record: `nat-on-education-private-schools-and-homeschooling`.

- **Legal term.** Private school, operating under an annual **Notice of
  Intention to Operate** (Education Act s.16, due 1 September). The ministry
  publishes the list of operating private schools (data.ontario.ca, updated
  October 2026) with each school's OSSD-credit status.
- **Oversight.** The ministry gives **no funding, regulation, licensing,
  accreditation or oversight of operations**. It inspects only high schools
  that seek or hold authority to grant OSSD credits. Elementary and
  non-credit schools are not inspected and need not follow the Ontario
  curriculum; teachers need not be certified. The ministry tells parents to
  do their own research.
- **Diploma.** The OSSD only through an inspected credit-granting private school
  (e.g., `school-on-vaughan-toronto-waldorf-school`,
  `school-on-ottawa-the-element-high-school`).
- **Homeschooling.** Education Act s.21(2)(a) excuses a child "receiving
  satisfactory instruction at home or elsewhere". PPM 131 asks parents to notify
  the board in writing before 1 September each year; boards accept the notice
  and normally do not investigate; no funding.
- **Online.** Private online schools are ordinary private schools; credit-granting
  ones are inspected (`school-on-bayfield-virtual-high-school`, BSID 665681).
- **Implication.** Easy to open, so Ontario has many small alternative schools
  (Waldorf, Montessori, forest schools) — and quality assurance rests entirely on
  the family's own checking for any school that does not grant credits.

## Québec

Records: `nat-qc-education-private-schools-and-home-schooling`,
`nat-qc-education-english-instruction-eligibility`.

- **Legal term.** Private educational institution holding a **permit** under the
  *Act respecting private education*; some are also **accredited for subsidies**
  (agréés aux fins de subventions). All must meet ministerial requirements on
  programs of study and pedagogical supervision; each sets its own admission
  rules. The ministry's open-data layer lists 353 private installations and their
  languages of instruction, but not their subsidy status.
- **Home schooling.** Notice to the Ministry's Direction de l'enseignement à la
  maison by **1 July** (or within ten days of leaving school); learning project
  implemented by **30 September**; monitoring; end-of-year report by 15 June;
  evaluation by a school service centre, a private school, a licensed teacher, a
  portfolio or a ministerial exam (Regulation respecting home schooling, in force
  1 July 2018).

### Language of instruction — what a newcomer family must know

Stated plainly, with sources dated 2026-10-30 (quebec.ca page updated
2026-10-28; Bill 104 text, 2002):

1. **The rule.** Preschool, elementary and secondary instruction in Québec is in
   **French**, except where the Charter of the French Language allows otherwise.
2. **Who may attend English school permanently.** A child who lives in Québec
   permanently **and whose mother or father is a Canadian citizen**, if one of
   four schooling-history conditions is met (English schooling in Canada of the
   child, of a sibling, or of a parent at elementary level, or a parent's
   schooling in Québec after 26 August 1977). Children of **permanent residents
   who are not yet citizens** do not meet this rule and attend **French** public
   or subsidised private schools. The ministry's leaflet for immigrant parents
   says: "Your children must therefore attend school in French until the end of
   their secondary studies."
3. **Temporary stays.** Children living in Québec temporarily — including
   dependants of foreign nationals holding a **work or study permit** — can
   obtain a **temporary authorisation** to attend English school. Special rules
   limit its duration; school-board and university sources report a maximum of
   three years and that permit-based authorisations are not renewable (these
   sources disagree on renewal — confirm with the ministry). It is not available
   to refugee claimants or to families who settle permanently.
4. **Unsubsidised private schools.** A private school that is **not accredited
   for subsidies** may teach in English to any child whose family pays full
   tuition. But since Bill 104 (2002), English instruction received there "by
   the child … or by a brother or sister of the child, shall be disregarded" when
   deciding eligibility for English public schooling. Since 2010 (s.73.1 and
   Regulation C-11, r. 2.1) such instruction can be weighed in a points-based
   assessment, but only for requests that already rest on a Canadian-citizen
   parent. **So an unsubsidised English private school is a lawful, fee-paying
   option, not a route into the English public system.**
5. **Subsidised private schools** (such as
   `school-qc-montreal-ecole-rudolf-steiner-de-montreal`) follow the same
   French-instruction rule as public schools.
6. **Appeals.** Administrative Tribunal of Québec; Examining Committee on the
   Language of Instruction for serious family or humanitarian situations.

Gaps: the consolidated Charter text (ss.72, 73, 73.1, 85) could not be fetched
(LégisQuébec and CanLII returned HTTP 403 twice); the reading of s.72 above is
labelled in the record as needing confirmation.

**Implication.** Unless the family arrives on temporary permits or a parent is a
Canadian citizen with the right schooling history, Québec means French-language
schooling, or full-fee unsubsidised English private school. Alternative models
exist in French (Waldorf in Montréal, Montessori secondary in Orford,
`school-qc-orford-ecole-secondaire-montessori-orford`) and Québec has a large
network of **public** alternative schools, which are out of scope
(`school-qc-montreal-ecole-le-vitrail`).

## Nova Scotia

Record: `nat-ns-education-private-schools-and-home-education`.

- **Legal term.** Private school with a curriculum "comparable to that provided
  by the public schools" (*Education Act* 2018, ss.85–87).
- **Funding.** None, except partial tuition support at designated
  special-education private schools.
- **Register and diploma.** The province publishes a list of **recognised private
  schools that grant the Nova Scotia High School Leaving Certificate** (open data,
  modified 2026-10-14) — e.g. `school-ns-lunenburg-class-afloat`,
  `school-ns-halifax-shambhala-school`. No public list of private elementary-only
  schools was found, so elementary schools such as
  `school-ns-halifax-halifax-independent-school` cannot be checked against a
  register.
- **Home education.** Annual registration form received by **20 September**;
  unfunded; the Minister may end a programme that does not meet the Act or where
  the child is not progressing; no credits or diploma from the home-schooling
  division; board courses possible with approval.

## Other provinces and territories (Phase 1: first pass only)

No `nat` records yet; facts below come from search results of official pages and
are **unverified** until read directly in Phase 2.

- **Manitoba.** Funded independent schools (Manitoba-certified teachers,
  provincial curriculum) and non-funded independent schools (visited annually,
  must give an education equivalent to public school; students do not receive the
  Manitoba diploma). Homeschooling by notification to the Homeschooling Office
  with progress reports in January and June. (edu.gov.mb.ca)
- **Saskatchewan.** Six categories of independent school (registered,
  alternative, associate, historical high, qualified, certified); registration
  with the ministry before admitting students; certified schools funded at 75 % of
  the average per-student rate (2022 release). Home-based education registered
  through the school division with a notice of intent and educational plan.
  (saskatchewan.ca; Provincial Auditor 2025)
- **New Brunswick.** Home schooling allowed where a child aged 5–18 receives
  "effective instruction"; annual application; fully home-schooled students are
  not eligible for the N.B. diploma. Private-school registration process not
  found. (gnb.ca)
- **Prince Edward Island.** *Private Schools Act* creates an Administrator of
  Private Schools; home education by annual Notice of Intent under the Education
  Act and Home Education Regulations. (princeedwardisland.ca)
- **Newfoundland and Labrador, Yukon, Northwest Territories, Nunavut.** Not
  researched in Phase 1 (N.W.T. has Private School Regulations under its
  Education Act). Parked; see `STATUS.md`.
