## 1. The law and its latest amendments

- **Charter of the French language** (1977): French is the official language of Quebec.
- **Bill 96**, *An Act respecting French, the official and common language of Québec*:
  - passed 24 May 2022 and assented 1 June 2022 (S.Q. 2022, c. 14);
  - **in force in stages: 1 June 2022, 1 June 2023 and 1 June 2025**;
  - made French the "common language";
  - created Francisation Québec and a French Language Commissioner;
  - extended francization duties to businesses with 25 or more employees;
  - restricted the use of other languages by the civil administration.
  - Some temporary measures under Bill 96 ended on **1 December 2025**, according to the Quebec
    immigration ministry's page as reported in search extracts.
- **Proposed, not law:** *Bill 1, Québec Constitution Act, 2025*.
  - introduced 9 October 2025;
  - adopted in principle 1 April 2026;
  - last listed at committee in June 2026;
  - would declare French the nation's sole common language.
  - Quebec held a general election on 5 October 2026, and news reports state the Parti Québécois
    formed a minority government. Whether Bill 1 lapsed with the dissolution of the legislature was
    not confirmed (`nat-qc-constitution-bill-1-2025`).

## 2. Public services: French by default (in force since 1 June 2023)

Quebec's provincial and municipal bodies must serve the public **exclusively in French** unless an
exception applies (`nat-qc-public-services-language`). The exceptions:

- **Immigrants:** another language may be used, *in addition to* French, for integration services
  **during the first six months after arrival**. After that, communications are expected to be in
  French.
- **People eligible for English instruction** (holders of a certificate of eligibility), and people
  who dealt with a body only in English before 13 May 2021, may be served in English.
- **Others:** Indigenous peoples, tourism services, and services outside Quebec.
- **Excluded:** children of temporary residents who receive a *temporary authorization* for English
  school do **not** thereby gain English communications.

**Practical effect.** A newcomer who does not read French should plan on translation help for
provincial correspondence (health insurance, licensing, the school board, the driver's licence)
after six months. Federal services (IRCC, CRA) are not covered by the Charter and remain available
in English.

## 3. Children's schooling (in force; Bill 96 amendments)

Source: `nat-qc-english-schooling-eligibility`.

- **The default is French school** for preschool, elementary and secondary education, in public and
  subsidised private schools.
- **English school eligibility** generally requires a **Canadian-citizen parent** plus a qualifying
  situation, such as English schooling in Canada. **Permanent-resident status alone does not give
  access.** Children of immigrants who settle permanently attend French school.
- **Temporary stays:** children of foreign nationals in Quebec temporarily to work or study may get a
  **temporary authorization** for English school. The Charter (s. 85) leaves the duration to
  regulation, and the official page says "special rules govern the maximum duration". Secondary
  sources (McGill's Family Care Office and reporting on Bill 96) describe **a maximum of 3 years,
  issued once and not renewable**, and never beyond the validity of the immigration document. This
  was not confirmed from the regulation itself, because LégisQuébec blocked automated access.
- **How to apply:** through the chosen school, which submits to the Ministry. Decisions usually take
  about 15 working days. Decisions can be contested before the Administrative Tribunal of Québec
  within 60 days.
- **Private unsubsidised schools, homeschooling and CEGEP** follow other rules. They belong to Agent
  03's lane, and this file flags them for Agent 03.

**Practical effect for the user's family** (children's ages unknown, question 3): settling
permanently in Quebec means French schooling for the children. Arriving as temporary workers gives
at most a limited period of English schooling, after which children would move to French schools
if the family stays. This is one reason Quebec may fail an essential requirement if the family
needs English or alternative schooling. That is a professional interpretation, to be confirmed with
Agent 03.

## 4. Language of work (in force)

Source: `nat-qc-language-of-work-and-francization`.

- Workers have a **right to carry out their activities in French**. All employers operating in
  Quebec, including federally regulated ones, must respect language-of-work obligations.
- Employers may require a language other than French only where justified. The detailed conditions
  (Charter s. 46) were not read in this pass.
- **Francization of businesses:**
  - 50+ employees: registered and certified under earlier rules;
  - 5–49 employees: must declare the share of staff who cannot work in French (since 1 June 2023);
  - **25–49 employees:** must register with the OQLF for francization since **1 June 2025**.
- **Practical effect:** English-only professional work exists, mainly in Montréal and in
  international firms, but French ability strongly affects employability. The Charter also links
  professional licensing to knowledge of French; that provision was not read in this pass.

## 5. Francization: learning French (in force)

Source: `nat-qc-francisation-quebec`.

- **Francisation Québec** is the single access point. Courses are **free** for people aged 16+ born
  outside Canada and living in Quebec, including temporary workers. Temporary students may join only
  if enrolled in a recognised programme. People planning to settle can also use the services.
- **Formats:** full-time sessions of 10 weeks at 25–30 hours a week, with incentive financial
  assistance; part-time and online formats, with no financial assistance for individual part-time
  learners since 23 September 2024.
- **Obligations:** no general legal duty on an individual newcomer to take French courses was found.
  French requirements bite through **immigration selection** (PSTQ French levels,
  `nat-qc-skilled-worker-selection-program`) and through the six-month rule for public services.

## 6. What this means for the decision

- If the household does not speak French, Quebec is a **high-friction choice**:
  - French-only provincial services after six months;
  - French schooling for children who settle;
  - French expected at work and by most regulators;
  - a provincial immigration route that requires French.
- If the household speaks good French, Quebec can be **advantageous**:
  - French-proficiency Express Entry rounds had the lowest cut-offs of the large rounds (382–435);
  - Francophone admission targets outside Quebec are rising;
  - Quebec's own programme is open to French speakers.
- Questions 4 and 8 decide this. Question 4 covers English and French ability. Question 8 is the
  bilingual-environment preference.
