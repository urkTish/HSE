## 1. The short version

- Canada will admit about **380,000 permanent residents a year in 2026–2028**. About 63% are
  economic immigrants. Federal high-skilled programmes (Express Entry) are targeted at 109,000 in
  2026 and provincial nominee programmes at 91,500 (`nat-ca-immigration-levels-plan-2026-2028`).
  **The 2027–2029 plan must be tabled by 1 November 2026**: re-check this week.
- **Express Entry now selects mostly people who are already in Canada or who fit a category.**
  Between August and October 2026 there were no all-programme rounds. Canadian Experience Class
  rounds cut off at 516–523 points. Category rounds cut off lower: French 382–435, trades and
  healthcare about 475. Provincial-nominee rounds cut off at 697–772 because a nomination adds 600
  points (`nat-ca-express-entry-2026`).
- **The ranking system changes on 3 November 2026 (announced).** Points will be given for Canadian
  work experience *or* a job offer, whichever gives more, and weighted towards high-wage
  occupations. An LMIA will no longer be needed for a job offer to count. Invitation rounds pause
  for up to two weeks while profiles are rescored (`nat-ca-express-entry-crs-changes-2026-11-03`).
- **For someone abroad without Canadian experience, three levers matter most:** a provincial
  nomination, a category the person fits (French, healthcare, trades, transport and others), and
  strong French. Without one of these, the federal route alone is currently weak. This is my
  reading of the draw pattern (professional interpretation), not an official statement.
- **Quebec selects its own economic immigrants (PSTQ) and requires French** for almost every stream
  (`nat-qc-skilled-worker-selection-program`). A Parti Québécois minority government took office
  after the 5 October 2026 election, so Quebec's targets for 2027 may change.

## 2. Express Entry (federal, in force)

Express Entry is an online pool, not a programme. It feeds three federal programmes and the
Express Entry-aligned provincial streams (`nat-ca-express-entry-2026`).

| Programme | Minimum work experience | Other key minimums |
|---|---|---|
| Federal Skilled Worker | 1 year continuous (1,560 h) in one TEER 0–3 occupation within 10 years, in Canada or abroad | CLB 7 in all four abilities; Educational Credential Assessment for foreign education; 67/100 on the FSW grid; proof of funds unless authorised to work in Canada with a job offer |
| Canadian Experience Class | 1 year (1,560 h) skilled TEER 0–3 work **in Canada** in the last 3 years | Language minimums by TEER (separate IRCC page, not read). Excludes work while a full-time student, self-employment and unauthorised work |
| Federal Skilled Trades | 2 years full-time in a skilled trade in the last 5 years | Language minimums; a 1-year full-time job offer or a Canadian certificate of qualification |

**How the Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) works (rules until 2 November 2026).** The maximum is
1,200 points. Core human capital is worth up to 500 points (460 with a spouse, plus up to 40 for
the spouse's factors). This covers age, education, language and Canadian work experience. Skill
transferability adds up to 100. Additional points add up to 600:

- provincial nomination: 600;
- French: 25 or 50;
- sibling in Canada: 15;
- Canadian post-secondary education: 15 or 30.

Job-offer points were removed in March 2025.

**From 3 November 2026 (announced; four days after this research).** A new "labour market
integration" factor is worth up to 150 points (140 with a spouse). A candidate gets the higher of
two scores:

- **Canadian work experience:** work-length points stay the same. A high-wage bonus of 70, 40 or
  20 points is added when the occupation's national median wage is at least 2.0×, 1.5× or 1.3× the
  average median wage.
- **A qualifying job offer:** 100, 70 or 50 points on the same wage tiers. An offer below 1.3×
  scores nothing.

LMIAs are no longer needed for a job offer to count. For Federal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled
Trades *eligibility*, the job offer can be in any TEER 0–3 job. Source:
`nat-ca-express-entry-crs-changes-2026-11-03`. **Implication:** a Canadian job offer in a well-paid
occupation becomes valuable again without an employer having to obtain an LMIA. The user's
occupation (unknown) now decides much more of the score.

**2026 category-based selection** (announced 18 February 2026):

- **New categories:**
  - physicians with Canadian work experience;
  - researchers and senior managers with Canadian work experience;
  - transport occupations;
  - skilled military recruits.
- **Continuing categories:** French-language proficiency, healthcare and social services, and
  trades.
- **STEM and education:** IRCC expects to meet these through general and programme rounds.

## 3. Provincial and territorial nominee programmes (in force)

Every province and territory except Quebec and Nunavut runs a Provincial Nominee Program (PNP).
The federal target for 2026 is 91,500 PNP admissions. An **Express Entry-aligned** nomination adds
600 CRS points. A **base** (paper) nomination leads to a separate federal application. Most streams
now need a job offer or prior work or study in the province, and many take applications by
invitation only. The streams listed are those relevant to skilled workers. They were read from
official pages on 2026-10-30, mostly through search extracts, so each record is `partially_verified`.

## 4. Family sponsorship (in force)

- **Spouses, partners and dependent children:** 69,000 admissions are planned for 2026. The sponsor
  must be a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, so this route is relevant only after the user
  has PR. Fees are $1,260 for a spouse with the right-of-PR fee and $180 per child
  (`nat-ca-ircc-fees-2026`, `nat-ca-family-sponsorship-2026`).
- **Parents and grandparents:** **intake has been paused since 15 July 2026**, so no new
  interest-to-sponsor forms are accepted. IRCC is processing existing files, with up to 15,000
  approvals planned in 2026. Processing took 23 months outside Quebec and 46 months for Quebec (as
  of January 2026). The super visa, for long visits, remains open.

## 5. Work permits and study-to-work (in force)

Source: `nat-ca-work-permits-and-study-to-work-2026`.

- **LMIA-based (Temporary Foreign Worker Program).** ESDC will not process *low-wage* LMIAs for jobs
  in census metropolitan areas with unemployment of 6% or more. The current list (9 October 2026
  to 7 January 2027) covers 30 of 42 areas, including Toronto, Montréal, Vancouver, Calgary,
  Edmonton, Ottawa–Gatineau, Halifax and Kelowna. High-wage LMIAs are not affected by this rule.
- **International Mobility Program (LMIA-exempt).** This covers trade-agreement permits,
  intra-company transfers, post-graduation work permits and spousal open work permits.
  - **Spousal open work permits:** available only if the principal worker is in TEER 0–1, or in
    TEER 2–3 in select occupations, and has at least 16 months left on their permit.
- **Study-to-work.** A post-graduation work permit needs CLB 7 for degree graduates and CLB 5 for
  other programmes. Non-degree programmes must be in a listed shortage field for study permits
  applied for from 1 November 2024. The usual sequence is then Canadian Experience Class or a PNP.
  Temporary-resident targets fall from 385,000 arrivals in 2026 to 370,000 in 2027 (155,000 then
  150,000 students), so study routes are tightening.

## 6. Levels Plan, fees and processing times

- **Levels Plan 2026–2028** (`nat-ca-immigration-levels-plan-2026-2028`):
  - 380,000 permanent residents a year;
  - economic 239,800, family 84,000, refugees 49,300 (2026);
  - Francophone admissions outside Quebec of 9% in 2026, rising to 10.5% in 2028.
  - About 115,000 protected persons and up to 33,000 work-permit holders are being transitioned to
    PR in 2026–2027.
- **Fees** (`nat-ca-ircc-fees-2026`): economic PR $1,590 per adult including the right-of-PR fee,
  $270 per child; work permit $155 plus $100 open-permit fee; study permit $150; biometrics $85
  ($170 per family); citizenship $653 per adult. Language tests, credential assessment, medicals
  and provincial fees are extra.
- **Processing times** (`nat-ca-ircc-processing-times-2026-10-29`): IRCC's permanent-residence times
  load dynamically and could not be read after two attempts, so they are recorded as **null**.
  Readable items: PR card 28 days; work-permit extension 123 days; study-permit extension 54 days.
  Work and study permits from abroad depend on the country of residence, which is unknown.

## 8. Risks and things to verify before any decision

1. **Rule churn is high.** In 2026 Ontario replaced its whole PNP, Express Entry's scoring changes on
   3 November, the parents-and-grandparents programme was paused, and a new Levels Plan is due within
   days. Any plan should be re-checked within 30 days of acting.
2. **Quebec's new government** (Parti Québécois minority, October 2026) may change immigration and
   language policy. The Quebec constitution bill (`nat-qc-constitution-bill-1-2025`) was only
   *proposed*.
3. **The federal non-Canadian buyer ban expires on 1 January 2027 unless extended.** Re-check in
   December 2026.
4. **Gaps (null) in this pass:** permanent-residence processing times; CLB minimums for CEC and FST;
   the occupations that count as high-wage; Atlantic Immigration Program details; the rural and
   Francophone community pilots.
